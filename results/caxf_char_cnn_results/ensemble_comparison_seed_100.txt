------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: RANKINGS & WINNER ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------

🏆 RANK 1 — WINNER: CCDS (Confidence-Calibrated Dynamic Selection)
  Macro F1        : 96.36%  (+20.08 pp over LCCDE)
  Macro Precision : 96.96%  (highest among all methods)
  DOM-based F1    : 0.89    (tied best with Stacking)
  Inference Time  : 0.67 s  (zero additional training required)
  Why Rank 1      : CCDS dominates across the most critical metrics in
                    imbalanced multi-class classification: Macro F1 and
                    Macro Precision. It uses Shannon entropy and
                    Jensen-Shannon Divergence to construct a continuous
                    disagreement spectrum, dynamically routing samples rather
                    than relying on naive hard votes.

🥈 RANK 2 — RUNNER-UP: BMA (Bayesian Model Averaging)
  Macro F1        : 95.23%  (+19.95 pp over LCCDE)
  Inference Time  : 0.08 s  (fastest alongside Stacking)
  Why Rank 2      : Achieves the highest raw accuracy (99.60%) and second-best
                    Macro F1 while running in just 80 milliseconds with zero
                    meta-training overhead. Best choice when low latency is required.

🥉 RANK 3 (TIED) — WSV (Weighted Soft Voting) & PCER (Per-Class Expert Routing)
  Macro F1        : 95.13%  (+19.85 pp over LCCDE)
  Why Rank 3      : Both methods significantly outperform LCCDE across all classes
                    and achieve 0.84 F1 on DOM-based XSS. In CharCNN's representation,
                    PCER's training precision table routes all four classes to
                    LightGBM as the expert, producing identical performance to
                    macro-F1 weighted soft voting.

RANK 5 — Stacking (Stacked Generalisation)
  Macro F1        : 93.27%  (+17.99 pp over LCCDE)
  Why Rank 5      : While Stacking achieves top-tier performance on DOM-based XSS
                    (F1=0.89), its Logistic Regression meta-learner suffers slightly
                    on Stored XSS precision (0.77). Because DOM-based XSS samples are
                    extremely rare in the OOF training folds (~4-5 instances per fold),
                    the linear meta-learner overcompensates on boundary thresholds.

RANK 6 — LCCDE (Baseline)
  Macro F1        : 75.28%  (Lowest overall performance)
  Why Rank 6      : LCCDE fails critically on DOM-based XSS (Precision = 7%, F1 = 0.13).
                    Because CharCNN features have relatively lower dimensionality
                    (410 vs TF-IDF's 5282), base models exhibit higher disagreement.
                    LCCDE's rigid hard-voting rules blindly delegate contested predictions
                    to a single confident model that is frequently wrong on rare classes.
